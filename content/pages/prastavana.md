title: "Prastāvanā"
title_devanagari: "प्रस्तावना"
gloss: "Preface"
---

the way triveṇī-saṅgamam is where the union of gaṅgā, yamunā and sarasvatī occurs, so does the union of vāk with its artha in a devotee's mind during sincere, steadfast adhyayana. thus, vāgartha-saṅgamaḥ is exactly what its name says; one such publicly accessible place where this union can be documented and revisited.

to me who reads by looking at every word and their many meanings and possible interpretations, and sits to think about what it means to embody the surrender so glorified and praised across thousands of texts praising the divine across its many forms—of the mother, the father, the beloved—this felt like the best way to document my long study sessions instead of losing them in long conversations and messy notes.

each piece studied is suffixed with -bodhinī (meaning "she who awakens"), which is the way commentaries were named; to hope for the true meaning and purpose of each text to be awakened within me every time i study them.

the dhātu-mañjarī is like the global aśvattha tree of root words and their usage across pieces i've studied; in the garlands of devotion that are these texts, if each word is a flower, the dhātus are the roots of the plants that yielded them, that caused the flower's existence. each such flower could make it into millions of garlands that adorn the divine only through the roots that grew them first. the dhātu-mañjarī resembles the branches that bridge the roots to the flowers, for our remembrance and understanding.

from the gut-wrenching poetry of gīta govindam to the hard-hitting plotlines of rāmāyaṇa and mahābhārata; from the profound truths of the upaniṣads to the compound praises and epithets of ślokas and stotras; hopefully the roots of my love for sanskrit and scripture branch out far and beyond to encompass all the kinds of bhagavan-nāma-smaraṇa there are.
