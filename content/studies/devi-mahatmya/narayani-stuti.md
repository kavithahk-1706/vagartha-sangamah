## Overview

The Nārāyaṇī Stuti is the hymn sung by the devas in Chapter 11 of the Devī Māhātmya, after the Devī has slain Śumbha and Niśumbha and the war is over. Unlike the Śakrādaya Stuti of Chapter 4 (post-Mahiṣāsura), which is predominantly awe-struck praise, the Nārāyaṇī Stuti is a theological map: it traces every form she took, classifies her aspects from the gentlest to the most ferocious, and concludes by dissolving all distinctions into her totality. Every verse ends with the refrain **नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते** (*nārāyaṇi namo'stu te*), a structural choice whose implications are explored in the Insights below.

The stuti is recited during Navadurgā pūjā, Caṇḍī pāṭha, and independently as a daily prayer. It is part of the Uttama Caritra (Chapters 5–13) attributed to Mahāsarasvatī.

## The arc

The stuti moves through four distinct registers:

1. **Universal-benevolent (vv. 8–12):** She is buddhi in all hearts, time that transforms all things, the auspicious Gaurī, the eternal śakti of the three guṇas, and the refuge of the surrendered. These are sarvabhāva epithets — vast, intimate, non-violent.

2. **Saptamātṛkā (vv. 13–19):** The seven Mothers, each mapped to a male deity's iconography but described as the active agent. The verbs escalate across the seven from ritual sprinkling (kṣarike) to the completed taking of life (prāṇahare), tracing a ferocity gradient from Brahmāṇī to Aindrī.

3. **Singular fierce forms (vv. 20–21):** Śivadūtī and Cāmuṇḍā. These sit outside the Saptamātṛkā unit. Śivadūtī is defined by a narrative act (sending Śiva as her messenger), and Cāmuṇḍā by her trophy-name (the slayer of Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa). Together they represent the apex of individuated ferocity.

4. **Omniform-return (vv. 22–23):** Lakṣmī, Lajjā, Mahāvidyā, Śraddhā, Puṣṭi, Svadhā, Mahārātri, Medhā, Sarasvatī, Tāmasī — she is all names at once. The triguṇātmikā totality is invoked: Mahārātri and Tāmasī map to Mahākālī (tamas/destruction), Lakṣmī and Puṣṭi to Mahālakṣmī (rajas/sustenance), Medhā and Sarasvatī to Mahāsarasvatī (sattva/knowledge). The stuti exhales from ferocity back into universality — but now the listener has seen what she is capable of, so the gentleness lands differently.

**The organizing principle is ferocity, not chronology.** In the Devī Māhātmya's own narrative, Cāmuṇḍā (Chapter 7: Caṇḍa-Muṇḍa vadha) precedes Śivadūtī (Chapter 8: Raktabīja). The stuti reverses this order because it groups by type and intensity, not by sequence of events. The Mātṛkās as a collective precede the individual fierce forms, and Cāmuṇḍā is placed last among the named forms because her very name encodes the kill.

## Verses

### 1. सर्वस्य बुद्धिरूपेण {#v1}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.8 · Metre: Anuṣṭup*

सर्वस्य बुद्धिरूपेण जनस्य हृदि संस्थिते ।
स्वर्गापवर्गदे देवि नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥

sarvasya buddhirūpeṇa janasya hṛdi saṃsthite |
svargāpavargade devi nārāyaṇi namo'stu te ||

**Padaccheda:** sarvasya · buddhi-rūpeṇa · janasya · hṛdi · saṃsthite · svarga-apavarga-de · devi · nārāyaṇi · namaḥ · astu · te

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| sarvasya | sarva · gen. sg. | of all |
| buddhi-rūpeṇa | buddhi (√budh) + rūpa · inst. sg. | in the form of intelligence |
| janasya | jana · gen. sg. | of people |
| hṛdi | hṛd · loc. sg. | in the heart |
| saṃsthite | sam + √sthā · past passive participle, fem. voc. sg. | (you who are) established, dwelling |
| svarga-apavarga-de | svarga + apavarga + √dā · fem. voc. sg. compound | O giver of heaven and liberation |
| devi | devī · fem. voc. sg. | O Goddess |
| nārāyaṇi | nārāyaṇī · fem. voc. sg. | O Nārāyaṇī |
| namaḥ | √nam · neut. nom. sg. | salutation |
| astu | √as · 3rd sg. imperative | may it be |
| te | tvam · dat. sg. | to you |

**Meaning:** O Devī who dwells in the hearts of all beings in the form of intelligence, O giver of both heaven and liberation — salutations to you, Nārāyaṇī.

**Context:** The opening verse of the Nārāyaṇī portion of the stuti. The devas begin at the most intimate register possible — not describing her cosmic form, but her presence inside every being. She is not distant; she is the buddhi you are using to read this verse.

**Insights:**
- This verse establishes that she is not merely the deity of a temple or a battlefield; she is the operative intelligence within every jīva. The Devī Māhātmya's first chapter demonstrates this: Viṣṇu cannot even open his eyes without her (as Yoganidrā) withdrawing. Here, the positive form: she IS the intelligence that makes thought possible.
- *svarga-apavarga-de*: she gives both bhoga (svarga, heavenly enjoyment) and mokṣa (apavarga, liberation). She is not exclusively a mokṣa-deity; the worldly and the transcendent are both in her gift.
- The vocative *saṃsthite* is crucial: "you who are established." Her dwelling in the heart is not an occasional visit but a permanent station.

**Cross-references:** Bhagavad Gītā 15.15, *mattaḥ smṛtir jñānam apohanaṃ ca* — "From Me come memory, knowledge, and their removal." The same theology: the divine is the source of both knowing and forgetting. See also DM 1.55–58, where Brahmā praises Mahāmāyā as the one who dwells in all beings as consciousness (cetanā), intelligence (buddhi), sleep (nidrā), hunger (kṣudhā), etc.

### 2. कलाकाष्ठादिरूपेण {#v2}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.9 · Metre: Anuṣṭup*

कलाकाष्ठादिरूपेण परिणामप्रदायिनि ।
विश्वस्योपरतौ शक्ते नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥

kalākāṣṭhādirūpeṇa pariṇāmapradāyini |
viśvasyoparatau śakte nārāyaṇi namo'stu te ||

**Padaccheda:** kalā-kāṣṭha-ādi-rūpeṇa · pariṇāma-pradāyini · viśvasya · uparatau · śakte · nārāyaṇi · namaḥ · astu · te

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| kalā-kāṣṭha-ādi-rūpeṇa | kalā + kāṣṭhā + ādi + rūpa · inst. sg. | in the form of kalā, kāṣṭhā, and other (units of time) |
| pariṇāma-pradāyini | pari + √nam + pra + √dā · fem. voc. sg. | O bestower of transformation |
| viśvasya | viśva · gen. sg. | of the universe |
| uparatau | upa + √ram · loc. sg. (abstract noun) | in the cessation / dissolution |
| śakte | śakti · fem. voc. sg. | O Power |
| nārāyaṇi | nārāyaṇī · fem. voc. sg. | O Nārāyaṇī |

**Meaning:** O bestower of all transformation, who takes the form of the smallest divisions of time — kalā, kāṣṭhā, and the rest — O Power behind the dissolution of the universe, salutations to you, Nārāyaṇī.

**Insights:**
- Kalā and kāṣṭhā are Vedic micro-units of time (a kāṣṭhā ≈ 3.2 seconds; a kalā = 30 kāṣṭhā). By naming the smallest increments, the verse says she is not only cosmic time (mahākāla) but granular time — every passing second is her form.
- *pariṇāma-pradāyini*: she who causes change. All transformation — aging, growth, decay, seasons, chemical reactions — is her function. She is not a goddess who intervenes in time; she IS time's capacity to transform things.
- *viśvasyoparatau śakte*: the power operative at the dissolution of the universe. The verse moves from the micro (kalā) to the macro (pralaya) in one breath.

### 3. सर्वमङ्गलमाङ्गल्ये {#v3}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.10 · Metre: Anuṣṭup*

सर्वमङ्गलमाङ्गल्ये शिवे सर्वार्थसाधिके ।
शरण्ये त्र्यम्बके गौरि नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥

sarvamaṅgalamāṅgalye śive sarvārthasādhike |
śaraṇye tryambake gauri nārāyaṇi namo'stu te ||

**Padaccheda:** sarva-maṅgala-māṅgalye · śive · sarva-artha-sādhike · śaraṇye · tri-ambake · gauri · nārāyaṇi · namaḥ · astu · te

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| sarva-maṅgala-māṅgalye | superlative compound, fem. voc. sg. | O most auspicious of all auspicious things |
| śive | śiva · fem. voc. sg. | O auspicious one |
| sarva-artha-sādhike | sarva + artha + √sādh · fem. voc. sg. | O accomplisher of all purposes |
| śaraṇye | śaraṇa + ya · fem. voc. sg. | O one worthy of refuge |
| tri-ambake | tri + ambaka · fem. voc. sg. | O three-eyed one |
| gauri | gaurī · fem. voc. sg. | O radiant / golden one |

**Meaning:** O most auspicious of all that is auspicious, O Śivā, O accomplisher of all aims, O worthy refuge, O three-eyed Gaurī — salutations to you, Nārāyaṇī.

**Insights:**
- This verse is among the most widely chanted individual ślokas in Hindu worship, often recited independently of the stuti.
- *sarvamaṅgalamāṅgalye*: a superlative built by compounding maṅgala (auspicious) with its own intensification. She is not one auspicious thing among many; she is the auspiciousness *of* auspicious things.
- *tryambake*: three-eyed. This is a Śaiva epithet (Śiva is Tryambaka), here applied directly to her. In the non-dual reading explored below, she does not borrow Śiva's attribute; Śiva's three-eyedness is hers, expressed through him.
- The density of vocatives in one verse (six names in two lines) makes this verse a compressed litany of her qualities.

### 4. सृष्टिस्थितिविनाशानाम् {#v4}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.11 · Metre: Anuṣṭup*

सृष्टिस्थितिविनाशानां शक्तिभूते सनातनि ।
गुणाश्रये गुणमये नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥

sṛṣṭisthitivināśānāṃ śaktibhūte sanātani |
guṇāśraye guṇamaye nārāyaṇi namo'stu te ||

**Padaccheda:** sṛṣṭi-sthiti-vināśānām · śakti-bhūte · sanātani · guṇa-āśraye · guṇa-maye · nārāyaṇi · namaḥ · astu · te

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| sṛṣṭi-sthiti-vināśānām | sṛṣṭi (√sṛj) + sthiti (√sthā) + vināśa (vi + √naś) · gen. pl. | of creation, sustenance, and destruction |
| śakti-bhūte | śakti + √bhū · fem. voc. sg. | O one who has become / who IS the power |
| sanātani | sanātana · fem. voc. sg. | O eternal one |
| guṇa-āśraye | guṇa + ā + √śri · fem. voc. sg. | O abode of the guṇas |
| guṇa-maye | guṇa + maya · fem. voc. sg. | O one composed of the guṇas |

**Meaning:** O eternal one, who IS the power behind creation, preservation, and destruction, O abode of the three guṇas, O one made of the guṇas — salutations to you, Nārāyaṇī.

**Insights:**
- *śaktibhūte*: not "possessor of śakti" but *bhūta* — having become, being. She does not have power; she IS the power. The distinction is ontological, not attributive.
- *guṇāśraye guṇamaye*: she is both the *āśraya* (support, ground) of the guṇas AND *maya* (pervaded by, made of) the guṇas. She transcends them and constitutes them simultaneously. This mirrors the Sāṅkhya-Vedānta synthesis: Prakṛti is the three guṇas, and she IS Prakṛti — not subordinate to a Puruṣa but herself the eternal (sanātanī) substratum.
- The verse maps onto the Trimūrti: sṛṣṭi → Brahmā, sthiti → Viṣṇu, vināśa → Śiva. She is the śakti of all three, or — in the non-dual reading — she IS all three, and their apparent separateness is her līlā.

### 5. शरणागतदीनार्त {#v5}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.12 · Metre: Anuṣṭup*

शरणागतदीनार्तपरित्राणपरायणे ।
सर्वस्यार्तिहरे देवि नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥

śaraṇāgatadīnārtaparitrāṇaparāyaṇe |
sarvasyārtihare devi nārāyaṇi namo'stu te ||

**Padaccheda:** śaraṇa-āgata-dīna-ārta-paritrāṇa-parāyaṇe · sarvasya · ārti-hare · devi · nārāyaṇi · namaḥ · astu · te

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| śaraṇa-āgata | śaraṇa + ā + √gam · bahuvrīhi | those who have come to (your) refuge |
| dīna-ārta | dīna + ārta | the wretched and the afflicted |
| paritrāṇa-parāyaṇe | pari + √trā + parāyaṇa · fem. voc. sg. | O one whose highest aim is the complete protection (of them) |
| sarvasya | sarva · gen. sg. | of all |
| ārti-hare | ārti + √hṛ · fem. voc. sg. | O remover of suffering |
| devi | devī · fem. voc. sg. | O Goddess |

**Meaning:** O you whose supreme purpose is the complete protection of the wretched and afflicted who have come to your shelter — O remover of all suffering, O Devī — salutations to you, Nārāyaṇī.

**Insights:**
- This is the last verse before the Saptamātṛkā section begins. It is the hinge: the universal-benevolent register closes with her as the protector of the surrendered, and the next verse opens with Brahmāṇī on her swan-chariot. The transition is from "she shelters" to "she fights."
- *parāyaṇe*: her *highest goal*, her *raison d'être*, is the protection of the sharaṇāgata. This word elevates protection from an attribute to a vocation.
- The compound *śaraṇāgatadīnārtaparitrāṇaparāyaṇe* is a single, unbroken vocative — one of the longest in the stuti. Its sheer length enacts what it describes: she wraps around the helpless the way this word wraps around every syllable within it.

### 6. हंसयुक्तविमानस्थे — Brahmāṇī {#v6}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.13 · Metre: Anuṣṭup*

हंसयुक्तविमानस्थे ब्रह्माणीरूपधारिणि ।
कौशाम्भःक्षरिके देवि नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥

haṃsayuktavimānasthe brahmāṇīrūpadhāriṇi |
kauśāmbhaḥkṣarike devi nārāyaṇi namo'stu te ||

**Padaccheda:** haṃsa-yukta-vimāna-sthe · brahmāṇī-rūpa-dhāriṇi · kauśa-ambhaḥ-kṣarike · devi · nārāyaṇi · namaḥ · astu · te

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| haṃsa-yukta-vimāna-sthe | haṃsa + yukta (√yuj) + vimāna + sthā · fem. voc. sg. | O one seated on a chariot yoked with swans |
| brahmāṇī-rūpa-dhāriṇi | brahmāṇī + rūpa + √dhṛ · fem. voc. sg. | O bearer of the form of Brahmāṇī |
| kauśa-ambhaḥ-kṣarike | kuśa + ambhas + √kṣar · fem. voc. sg. | O one who sprinkles holy water (with kuśa grass) |
| devi | devī · fem. voc. sg. | O Goddess |

**Meaning:** O you who are seated on a chariot yoked with swans, who bear the form of Brahmāṇī, O Devī who sprinkles the sacred water — salutations to you, Nārāyaṇī.

**Context:** The first of the Saptamātṛkā verses. Brahmāṇī carries Brahmā's iconography: the haṃsa (swan), the vimāna, and the kamaṇḍalu for sprinkling consecrated water. She performs a ritual, purificatory function — creation and sanctification rather than combat.

**Insights:**
- *kṣarike*: from √kṣar, to flow or sprinkle. This is the gentlest verb in the entire Mātṛkā sequence. No weapon, no violence — only the flowing of sacred water. The ferocity gradient begins here at zero.
- *rūpa-dhāriṇi*: "bearer of the form." The form is something she puts on, not something she is constituted by. Brahmā's appearance is a costume she wears. This language supports the non-dual reading (see Bodhāḥ): she is not the śakti *within* Brahmā; she IS Brahmā, wearing that form.

**Bodhāḥ:** [on *kā* not *kaḥ*](#r1), [on ferocity gradient](#r5), [on the non-dual reading](#r6)

### 7. त्रिशूलचन्द्राहिधरे — Māheśvarī {#v7}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.14 · Metre: Anuṣṭup*

त्रिशूलचन्द्राहिधरे महावृषभवाहिनि ।
माहेश्वरीस्वरूपेण नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥

triśūlacandrāhidhare mahāvṛṣabhavāhini |
māheśvarīsvarūpeṇa nārāyaṇi namo'stu te ||

**Padaccheda:** tri-śūla-candra-ahi-dhare · mahā-vṛṣabha-vāhini · māheśvarī-svarūpeṇa · nārāyaṇi · namaḥ · astu · te

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| tri-śūla | tri + śūla | trident |
| candra | candra | crescent moon |
| ahi | ahi | serpent |
| dhare | √dhṛ · fem. voc. sg. | O bearer / holder |
| mahā-vṛṣabha-vāhini | mahā + vṛṣabha + √vah · fem. voc. sg. | O one who rides the great bull |
| māheśvarī-svarūpeṇa | māheśvarī + svarūpa · inst. sg. | in the essential form of Māheśvarī |

**Meaning:** O bearer of the trident, the crescent moon, and the serpent, O rider of the great bull — in the very nature of Māheśvarī, salutations to you, Nārāyaṇī.

**Insights:**
- Every signature element of Śiva is packed into one line: triśūla (weapon), candra (hair ornament), ahi (neck ornament), mahāvṛṣabha (Nandī, vehicle). She carries his entire iconographic identity.
- *dhare*: "bearer" — she holds these, actively. Same verb as Brahmāṇī's section but applied to weapons and ornaments rather than holy water. The ferocity has begun to escalate: she now holds a trident.
- *svarūpeṇa* rather than *rūpadhāriṇi*: for Brahmāṇī, she "bears the form" (rūpa-dhāriṇi); for Māheśvarī, she is "in the svarūpa" — the essential nature. Māheśvarī is not a costume; it is a svarūpa, a self-form.

### 8. मयूरकुक्कुटवृते — Kaumārī {#v8}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.15 · Metre: Anuṣṭup*

मयूरकुक्कुटवृते महाशक्तिधरेऽनघे ।
कौमारीरूपसंस्थाने नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥

mayūrakukkuṭavṛte mahāśaktidhare'naghe |
kaumārīrūpasaṃsthāne nārāyaṇi namo'stu te ||

**Padaccheda:** mayūra-kukkuṭa-vṛte · mahā-śakti-dhare · anaghe · kaumārī-rūpa-saṃsthāne · nārāyaṇi · namaḥ · astu · te

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| mayūra | mayūra | peacock |
| kukkuṭa | kukkuṭa | rooster |
| vṛte | √vṛ · fem. voc. sg. (past passive participle) | O one surrounded by |
| mahā-śakti-dhare | mahā + śakti + √dhṛ · fem. voc. sg. | O bearer of the great spear (Vel/Śakti) |
| anaghe | an-agha · fem. voc. sg. | O sinless one |
| kaumārī-rūpa-saṃsthāne | kaumārī + rūpa + sam + √sthā · fem. voc. sg. | O one established in the form of Kaumārī |

**Meaning:** O one surrounded by peacocks and roosters, O bearer of the great Śakti-spear, O sinless one, O one established in the form of Kaumārī — salutations to you, Nārāyaṇī.

**Insights:**
- Kumāra/Skanda's two birds: the mayūra (his vehicle) and the kukkuṭa (on his battle-banner). The mahāśakti is his vel/spear.
- *anaghe*: sinless, blemishless. This is the only Mātṛkā verse that includes a moral quality alongside the iconographic ones. Kumāra is the eternally young brahmacārin — and his śakti carries that purity as an intrinsic attribute, not just weapons and vehicles.
- *dhare* again: she bears the spear. Still holding, not yet wielding. The verb has not escalated past Māheśvarī.
- In the non-dual reading: she is not "the śakti within the sinless one." She IS the sinless one. She IS the youth. She IS Kaumārī — eternally young and always pure.

### 9. शङ्खचक्रगदाशार्ङ्ग — Vaiṣṇavī {#v9}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.16 · Metre: Anuṣṭup*

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |

- *prasīda*: the only Mātṛkā verse with a direct plea. The devas are not just praising; they are asking for grace. Viṣṇu's relationship with devotees is grace-based (anugraha), and the bhakti register enters the stuti specifically at this verse.
- *paramāyudhe*: supreme weapons. Not just divine weapons — the highest weapons. All four simultaneously, one in each of four arms.

**Cross-references:** The coexistence of Vaiṣṇavī (v. 16) and Nārāyaṇī (refrain) in the same verse is a key argument for the non-Vaiṣṇava reading of the name Nārāyaṇī. See the section on the refrain below.

## On the refrain: Why "Nārāyaṇī"?

The name Nārāyaṇī appears after every verse — after Brahmāṇī and Māheśvarī and Cāmuṇḍā alike. A surface reading takes it as "wife/śakti of Nārāyaṇa (Viṣṇu)." But this creates a redundancy: Vaiṣṇavī already appears as a separate Mātṛkā (v. 16). If Nārāyaṇī simply meant "she who belongs to Nārāyaṇa," the refrain would be doing theological double-entry bookkeeping on the Vaiṣṇavī verse and would be silent — even contradictory — on the Māheśvarī and Cāmuṇḍā verses.

The universality of the refrain demands a universal meaning for the name. The argument:

1. Nārāyaṇa (nāra + ayana) means "the refuge/shelter of all beings (nāra)." Nārāyaṇa is called Nārāyaṇa because he performs this function.
2. Nārāyaṇī, as a feminine form of the same compound, means "she who IS the refuge of all beings" — not derived from the god, but sharing the etymological root directly.
3. In the śākta reading: Nārāyaṇa gets to be the refuge because Nārāyaṇī (the śakti within him) is what enables him to function as such. She is the source of the quality that gives him his name.
4. The causal arrow is reversed: she is not Nārāyaṇī because she belongs to Nārāyaṇa; Nārāyaṇa is Nārāyaṇa because she is within him.

This reading is consistent with DM Chapter 1, where Viṣṇu is inoperative (asleep) until she withdraws, and with DM 10.5 (*ekaivaham*), where she denies the existence of anyone other than herself.

## On the non-dual reading of the Mātṛkās

A common reading of the Saptamātṛkā verses is that the Devī is the śakti *within* each male deity — the power that enables Indra to slay Vṛtra, Viṣṇu to lift the earth, and so on.

A deeper reading, supported by the grammar and the theology of the text, reverses this: she IS each deity, wearing that form. The evidence:

1. **Grammar:** The subject of every verse is always her. *Brahmāṇīrūpadhāriṇi* — she who bears the form of Brahmāṇī. The form is something she puts on. The verbs — kṣarike, dhare, gṛhīta, prāṇahare — are all attributed to her, not to a god she indwells.
2. **Reabsorption (DM 10.3–5):** When Śumbha accuses her of relying on others' strength, she reabsorbs every Mātṛkā back into herself and declares *ekaivaham jagaty atra*. If the Mātṛkās were separate beings powered by her śakti, the reabsorption would diminish them. Instead, they simply stop being differentiated — they were always her.
3. **Theological parallel:** Just as one says "in the form of Kālī," "in the form of Sarasvatī" — implying one śakti taking different forms — the stuti says "in the form of Vaiṣṇavī," "in the form of Māheśvarī." The male gods are not containers she inhabits; they are forms she wears.
4. **The Aindrī proof:** *Vṛtraprāṇahare* — she took Vṛtra's prāṇa. Vṛtra-vadha is Indra's defining act, his very identity. The stuti does not say "she empowered Indra to slay Vṛtra." It says *she* took Vṛtra's life-breath. If she is the actor, Indra is the form.

> **2.** {#r2} (on the stuti as a whole; relates to [v. 5](#v5)) "The gods don't call their own kāryas something she needs to bless. They don't say 'please give us your grace so we can do our kārya.' They say 'this is YOUR kārya.' Because she is the doer within everyone."

> **3.** {#r3} (on the refrain; relates to [v. 9, Vaiṣṇavī](#v9)) "She's called Nārāyaṇī because she is the refuge. If we go a step further, Nārāyaṇa gets to be the refuge because Nārāyaṇī is the śakti within him that lets him be Nārāyaṇa. It's actually the reverse."

> **4.** {#r4} (on [v. 12, Aindrī](#v12)) "For the longest time I looked at *sahasranayanoijvale* and thought it was just some big adjective I don't understand. I never parsed it as *vṛtraprāṇahare*, as in 'the one who takes the life of Vṛtra.' Sanskrit was supposed to be ununderstandable. I wasn't even listening, probably."

> **5.** {#r5} (on [vv. 6–12, Mātṛkā verb gradient](#v6)) "It's not saying Brahmāṇī doesn't kill anyone and Vaiṣṇavī is where you start being ferocious. This is the specific order of the Saptamātṛkā and it always stays like this. The people who wrote this are choosing to illustrate these forms in a specific order, in increasing ferocity."

> **6.** {#r6} (on [vv. 6–12, the non-dual reading](#v6)) "These verses aren't saying she's the one within them making them do it. They're very active. They're attributing all of these things to her. She IS Brahmā when she pours the water. She IS Māheśvarī when she bears the trident. She IS the sinless one — not the one within the sinless one."

> **7.** {#r7} (on [vv. 15–16, Tāmasī and Mahārātri](#v15)) "Even the thing you're scared of, even the thing that is deluding you, even the thing that is supposed to be your greatest fear — even that is not different from them. When it's not different from them, surrendering doesn't feel like this big duel."

> **8.** {#r8} (on [v. 15, Mahāvidyā](#v15)) "She's Mahāvidyā because she is parāvidyā. Everything else — the Purāṇas, the stories, the ślokas, the sūtras, the tantras — is used to get to her. If you are practicing it but you don't actually get to her, it's not fulfilling any purpose."

> **9.** {#r9} (on the stuti as a whole) "The one thing no one should ever do in śakti sādhanā is speak on behalf of her. She's perfectly capable of speaking to whoever she wants to speak to."

> **10.** {#r10} (on [vv. 15–16, Tāmasī](#v16)) "Is it really that bad that she comes and wreaks havoc in your life? Because even that can be presence."

> **11.** {#r11} (on [v. 5, śaraṇāgati](#v5) and DM 1.55, *balāt ākṛṣya*) "It's like your mother is telling you a story to put you to sleep, and she keeps talking about scary things like ogres and monsters. You're like 'amma, I'm scared,' and then she just stops and holds you. That's it. That's the whole sādhanā."

## Open threads

1. **Kauśāmbhaḥkṣarike** (v. 13, Brahmāṇī): the exact compound analysis needs verification. Is *kauśa* from kuśa-grass, or from kośa (treasury/sheath)? The reading *kauśāmbhaḥ* (water from kuśa-grass) is standard but a textual note is warranted.
2. **Bābhravī** (v. 23): further study needed on whether this is a colour-epithet (tawny), a reference to a specific form, or connected to Babhru as an epithet of Viṣṇu/Śiva.
3. **Mahāvidyā appearing twice** (v. 22): intentional emphasis, or one instance referring to parāvidyā and the other to the Daśamahāvidyā tradition? Worth exploring with commentary traditions.
4. **The Lalitopākhyāna parallels:** the Bāla episode (kūrcaprasarakomalam, sāśrukā) as the emotional counterpart to the Devī Māhātmya's martial theology — worth a comparative note.
