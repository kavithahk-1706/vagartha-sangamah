## Overview

The Saptaślokī Durgā is the "pocket Saptaśatī": seven verses lifted from the 700-verse Devī Māhātmya, framed by a short dialogue in which **Śiva asks and Devī answers**. Śiva wants an *upāya*, a means for success in Kali-yuga. Out of affection for him, Devī reveals this hymn to herself.

All seven verses are taken whole from the main text. Each one keeps its original metre, so the stotra switches metres as it goes, like a greatest-hits compilation where every track keeps its own time signature.

The three deities named in the viniyoga, Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī and Mahāsarasvatī, are the presiding deities of the three *caritras* of the full Saptaśatī. So the seven verses stand in for all seven hundred.

This page records a word-by-word **adhyayana** (study pass), not the formal *pāṭha*. The text was studied with interruptions to understand it before reciting it whole.

## The arc

1. **She binds.** Mahāmāyā drags even the minds of the wise into moha (v1).
2. **She softens.** Remembered in fear, she removes fear. Remembered in ease, she gives clear understanding. Her heart is always melting (v2).
3. **The devas just keep calling her name.** A verse that is nothing but vocatives and a single tiny verb (v3).
4. **She is devoted to rescuing the helpless** (v4).
5. **She is everything**, so save us from fear (v5). This is the first actual plea.
6. **Refuge in her means no final ruin**, and the sheltered become shelter (v6).
7. **It is her kārya. She does it** (v7).

**Structural bookends:**

- **Kārya at both ends.** Śiva's opening question asks for *kārya*-siddhi, success in what must be done. The final verse ends with *tvayā **kāryam***, "it is to be done *by you*." The whole stotra moves from "how do I succeed?" to "you are the doer."
- **Mati taken, mati given.** In v1 she drags the *mati* of the wise into moha. In v2 she gives *śubhā mati*.
- **Moha bound and unbound.** v1 hands minds over to moha. v7 asks her to destroy "our enemies," and moha is one of the six inner enemies (devotional reading).
- **The scope keeps widening.** *aśeṣa-jantoḥ* (v2), *sarva-upakāra* (v2), *sarvasya ārti-hare* (v4): every creature, everyone's suffering.

## Preliminaries

### Śiva's question {#p1}
*Framing dialogue · Metre: anuṣṭup*

शिव उवाच
देवि त्वं भक्तसुलभे सर्वकार्यविधायिनी।
कलौ हि कार्यसिद्ध्यर्थमुपायं ब्रूहि यत्नतः॥

*śiva uvāca*
*devi tvaṃ bhaktasulabhe sarvakāryavidhāyinī*
*kalau hi kāryasiddhyartham upāyaṃ brūhi yatnataḥ*

**Padaccheda:** devi | tvam | bhakta-sulabhe | sarva-kārya-vidhāyinī | kalau | hi | kārya-siddhi-artham | upāyam | brūhi | yatnataḥ

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| śiva uvāca | √vac · perfect, 3rd sg. | Śiva said |
| devi | vocative | O Devī |
| tvam | pronoun, nom. | you |
| bhakta-sulabhe | su + √labh · vocative | O one easily attained by devotees (sulabha = easy to get; opposite durlabha) |
| sarva-kārya-vidhāyinī | vi + √dhā · fem. agent noun | accomplisher of all undertakings |
| kalau | loc. of kali | in Kali-yuga |
| hi | particle | indeed |
| kārya-siddhi-artham | √sidh (siddhi) · "for the sake of" | for success in one's works |
| upāyam | acc. | a means, a method |
| brūhi | √brū · imperative 2nd sg. | tell me |
| yatnataḥ | √yat · adverb | carefully, properly |

**Meaning:** O Devī, you who are easily reached by your devotees and who accomplish every undertaking, please tell me carefully: what is the means for success in Kali-yuga?

**Insights:**
- **The roles are flipped.** In most tantric texts Devī asks and Śiva teaches. Here Śiva is the student and she is the source. Even Mahādeva comes to her for the upāya.
- **He praises before he asks.** The first thing he says about her is *bhakta-sulabhe*: she is easy to reach for those who love her. The epithets explain why he asks *her*: she gets things done and she is easy to reach.

**Reading notes:** Kavitha's first summary ("he's asking her to explain properly what the solution is for kārya-siddhi in Kali-yuga") was correct, but it skipped the epithets. They matter, because they give the reason for asking her.

### Devī's answer {#p2}
*Framing dialogue · Metre: anuṣṭup*

देव्युवाच
शृणु देव प्रवक्ष्यामि कलौ सर्वेष्टसाधनम्।
मया तवैव स्नेहेनाप्यम्बास्तुतिः प्रकाश्यते॥

*devy uvāca*
*śṛṇu deva pravakṣyāmi kalau sarveṣṭasādhanam*
*mayā tavaiva snehenāpy ambāstutiḥ prakāśyate*

**Padaccheda:** śṛṇu | deva | pravakṣyāmi | kalau | sarva-iṣṭa-sādhanam | mayā | tava | eva | snehena | api | ambā-stutiḥ | prakāśyate

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| devī uvāca | √vac · perfect, 3rd sg. | Devī said |
| śṛṇu | √śru · imperative 2nd sg. | listen (same root as śruti) |
| deva | vocative | O Deva |
| pravakṣyāmi | pra + √vac · future 1st sg. | I will declare |
| kalau | loc. | in Kali-yuga |
| sarva-iṣṭa-sādhanam | √iṣ (iṣṭa), √sādh (sādhana) · neut. acc. sg. | the means of achieving every desired thing |
| mayā | instr. | by me |
| tava eva | gen. + particle | for you alone |
| snehena | instr. of sneha | out of affection |
| api | particle | indeed |
| ambā-stutiḥ | nom. | the hymn of praise to Ambā, the Mother |
| prakāśyate | pra + √kāś · passive present 3rd sg. | is being revealed, literally "brought into the light" |

**Meaning:** Listen, Deva, I will tell you the means to achieve every desire in Kali-yuga. Purely out of my affection for you, this hymn to the Mother is now revealed by me.

**Insights:**
- **She gives more than he asked for.** He asked for *kārya-siddhi*, success in tasks. She answers with *sarva-iṣṭa-sādhana*, the means to every desire.
- **The reason is sneha, affection.** It isn't formality and it isn't a reward for tapas.
- **The hymn she reveals praises herself.** The *mayā … prakāśyate* construction makes it sound like something hidden being lit up, a secret being opened.

### Viniyoga {#p3}

ॐ अस्य श्रीदुर्गासप्तश्लोकीस्तोत्रमन्त्रस्य नारायण ऋषिः, अनुष्टुप् छन्दः, श्रीमहाकालीमहालक्ष्मीमहासरस्वत्यो देवताः, श्रीदुर्गाप्रीत्यर्थं सप्तश्लोकीदुर्गापाठे विनियोगः।

*oṃ asya śrīdurgāsaptaślokīstotramantrasya nārāyaṇa ṛṣiḥ, anuṣṭup chandaḥ, śrīmahākālīmahālakṣmīmahāsarasvatyo devatāḥ, śrīdurgāprītyarthaṃ saptaślokīdurgāpāṭhe viniyogaḥ*

| Word | Grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| asya | gen. of idam | of this |
| śrī-durgā-sapta-ślokī-stotra-mantrasya | gen. | of the Śrī Durgā seven-verse stotra-mantra |
| nārāyaṇa ṛṣiḥ | nom. | Nārāyaṇa is the seer |
| anuṣṭup chandaḥ | nom. | the metre is anuṣṭup |
| śrī-mahākālī-mahālakṣmī-mahāsarasvatyaḥ devatāḥ | nom. pl. | the deities are Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī and Mahāsarasvatī |
| śrī-durgā-prīti-artham | "for the sake of" | for the delight of Śrī Durgā |
| sapta-ślokī-durgā-pāṭhe | loc. | in the recitation of the Saptaślokī Durgā |
| viniyogaḥ | nom. | the application ("this is being used for this purpose") |

**Meaning:** Of this Saptaślokī Durgā stotra, Nārāyaṇa is the ṛṣi, anuṣṭup is the metre, and Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī and Mahāsarasvatī are the deities. It is applied in this recitation for the pleasure of Śrī Durgā.

**Insights:**
- **The standard trio.** Every viniyoga names ṛṣi, chandas and devatā. Traditionally you touch the head for the ṛṣi, the mouth for the chandas and the heart for the devatā, placing the seer, the sound and the deity on your own body before you begin.
- **Bīja, śakti and kīlaka** appear in the viniyogas of mantras with seed syllables, such as Śrīvidyā mantras and the full Saptaśatī's caritras. Plain stotras usually stop at the trio.
- **The three devatās are the three caritras** of the full Saptaśatī: prathama is Mahākālī, madhyama is Mahālakṣmī, uttama is Mahāsarasvatī.
- **The ṛṣi is Nārāyaṇa.** Śiva asks, Devī reveals, and Nārāyaṇa is the seer.
- **"Anuṣṭup" is the conventional label.** Not every verse is actually in anuṣṭup (see Metre notes).

**Reading notes:** The Gita Press text (followed here) reads **anuṣṭup chandaḥ**. Other editions read **anuṣṭubādīni chandāṃsi**, "anuṣṭup *and others* are the metres." Kavitha's view: the variant is technically more accurate, since the stotra also contains vasantatilakā (v2) and indravajrā (v6).

## Verses

### 1. jñāninām api cetāṃsi {#v1}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 1.55 · Metre: anuṣṭup*

ज्ञानिनामपि चेतांसि देवी भगवती हि सा।
बलादाकृष्य मोहाय महामाया प्रयच्छति॥

*jñāninām api cetāṃsi devī bhagavatī hi sā*
*balād ākṛṣya mohāya mahāmāyā prayacchati*

**Padaccheda:** jñāninām | api | cetāṃsi | devī | bhagavatī | hi | sā | balāt | ākṛṣya | mohāya | mahāmāyā | prayacchati

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| jñāninām | √jñā · gen. pl. of jñānin | of the wise, of those who know |
| api | particle | even |
| cetāṃsi | √cit · acc. pl. of cetas (n.) | minds |
| devī bhagavatī | nom. | the blessed Goddess |
| hi | particle | indeed |
| sā | pronoun, nom. fem. | she |
| balāt | abl. of bala, used as adverb | by force |
| ākṛṣya | ā + √kṛṣ · gerund (lyap) | having dragged, pulled |
| mohāya | √muh · moha, masc. dat. sg. (of purpose) | into moha, toward delusion |
| mahāmāyā | nom. | the Great Māyā |
| prayacchati | pra + √yam · present 3rd sg. parasmaipada | hands over, delivers |

**Meaning:** That blessed Devī Mahāmāyā forcibly drags even the minds of the wise and hands them over to delusion.

**Context:** King Suratha has lost his kingdom, and Samādhi the merchant has been thrown out by his own family. Both *know* their losses aren't worth grieving over, yet they keep grieving. They ask Medhas Ṛṣi why, and this verse is his answer: knowing better does not save you, because she is stronger than your knowing. Medhas then narrates the entire Saptaśatī to the two of them.

**Insights:**
- **Why open a wish-granting stotra with "she will overpower your mind"?** Because the next verse in chapter 1 says the same Mahāmāyā, when pleased, grants mukti (*saiṣā prasannā varadā nṛṇāṃ bhavati muktaye*). The one who binds is the one who frees. The verse sets the ground rule: the intellect cannot get you out, only she can. It's humility before asking.
- **Pride is the verse working in real time.** The small swell of "I know things" is itself the moha. It isn't a separate problem to fix first. Swinging to "my knowledge is not even a speck" is the same ego performing humility. The point is not to rank how much you know, but to see that knowing isn't what holds you up.
- **Suratha and Samādhi's response was a practice, not a mindset fix.** They went to a riverbank, made an image of her, and worshipped for three years. In practice: notice the pride, don't fight it, hand it back, and keep going.
- **ākṛṣya and Kṛṣṇa.** √kṛṣ ("to draw, attract") is the root traditionally linked to *kṛṣṇa*, the one who draws everything to himself. She draws minds *into* moha. He draws hearts *toward* himself. The same root pulls in two directions.

**Cross-references:**
- Gītā 7.14, *daivī hy eṣā guṇamayī mama māyā duratyayā*. Krishna calls it "MY māyā": it can't be beaten, only surrendered to its owner.
- Gītā 7.17. The jñānī is dearest, but specifically the *eka-bhakti* one, whose knowing ends in devotion.
- Ayi giri nandini: *suratha-samādhi samāna-samādhi…*, the same two characters from this chapter.

**Reading notes:** Kavitha's reading was correct: "Devī Bhagavatī Mahāmāyā herself forcibly drags even the most knowledgeable minds into moha." Her refinement was that knowledge doesn't *cause* grace. It runs parallel to it and can't be the thing that leads to it. One correction: knowledge isn't useless. It is welcome as an offering when it serves devotion, and it becomes moha's handle when it is held as possession.

### 2. durge smṛtā harasi {#v2}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 4.17, Śakrādi-stuti · Metre: vasantatilakā*

दुर्गे स्मृता हरसि भीतिमशेषजन्तोः
स्वस्थैः स्मृता मतिमतीव शुभां ददासि।
दारिद्र्यदुःखभयहारिणि का त्वदन्या
सर्वोपकारकरणाय सदार्द्रचित्ता॥

*durge smṛtā harasi bhītim aśeṣajantoḥ*
*svasthaiḥ smṛtā matim atīva śubhāṃ dadāsi*
*dāridryaduḥkhabhayahāriṇi kā tvadanyā*
*sarvopakārakaraṇāya sadārdracittā*

**Padaccheda:** durge | smṛtā | harasi | bhītim | aśeṣa-jantoḥ | svasthaiḥ | smṛtā | matim | atīva | śubhām | dadāsi | dāridrya-duḥkha-bhaya-hāriṇi | kā | tvat-anyā | sarva-upakāra-karaṇāya | sadā | ārdra-cittā

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| durge | vocative | O Durgā |
| smṛtā | √smṛ · past passive participle, fem. | when remembered |
| harasi | √hṛ · present 2nd sg. parasmaipada | you take away (same root as Hari) |
| bhītim | √bhī · bhīti, fem. acc. sg. | fear |
| aśeṣa-jantoḥ | gen. sg. | of every creature without exception (a-śeṣa = without remainder) |
| svasthaiḥ | √sthā (sva-stha) · instr. pl. | by those who are well, "established in themselves" |
| smṛtā | √smṛ · ppp. fem. | when remembered |
| matim | √man · mati, fem. acc. sg. | understanding, intellect |
| atīva | adverb | exceedingly |
| śubhām | acc. fem. | auspicious |
| dadāsi | √dā · present 2nd sg. parasmaipada | you give |
| dāridrya-duḥkha-bhaya-hāriṇi | √hṛ · vocative | O remover of poverty, sorrow and fear |
| kā | interrogative, nom. fem. | who? |
| tvat-anyā | nom. fem. | other than you |
| sarva-upakāra-karaṇāya | upa + √kṛ, √kṛ · neut. dat. sg. (of purpose) | for doing good to all |
| sadā | adverb | always |
| ārdra-cittā | √cit · fem. nom. sg., bahuvrīhi | whose heart is moist, melting, soft (ārdra = wet) |

**Meaning:** O Durgā, remembered in distress you take away the fear of every being, and remembered by those at ease you grant a supremely auspicious understanding. O remover of poverty, sorrow and fear, who other than you has a heart that is always melting to do good to all?

**Context:** Chapter 4, after Mahiṣāsura's death. Indra and the devas praise her.

**Insights:**
- **Two kinds of remembering, and both count.** Remember her afraid and she takes the fear. Remember her while at ease and she gives clear understanding. There is no requirement to remember her "correctly" or in a calm state. *Smṛtā* is the entire requirement.
- **It bookends v1.** There, mahāmāyā drags the mind of the wise into moha. Here she *gives* śubhā mati. The same goddess takes the mind away and gives it back clean.
- **ārdra-cittā.** Her heart isn't just kind, it is wet and always melting, like clay that takes a thumbprint. *kā tvad-anyā*, "who else is like this?", is not really a question.
- **The scope has no entry requirement.** Her heart melts for *sarva*, all, and back up top for *aśeṣa-jantu*, every creature without exception. Surrender is how you turn around and *notice* the good she is already pouring out. It is not a ticket that switches her kindness on. Her heart was wet before you showed up.
- **"It's all her will" should be comforting.** If a cold force ran everything, "nothing is in my control" would be terrifying. But the one running everything is *sadā ārdra-cittā*, so "not in my control" becomes "in hands that are always soft."

**Reading notes:** Kavitha first read the last half as "Who is dāridrya-duḥkha-bhaya-hāriṇī other than you? Your heart is always melting to do good for everyone." She then caught that *dāridrya-duḥkha-bhaya-hāriṇi* is **vocative** (short final -i), so it addresses her and isn't the subject of "who else." The *kā tvad-anyā* ("who else") attaches to the melting heart.

### 3. sarvamaṅgalamāṅgalye {#v3}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.10, Nārāyaṇī-stuti · Metre: anuṣṭup*

सर्वमङ्गलमाङ्गल्ये शिवे सर्वार्थसाधिके।
शरण्ये त्र्यम्बके गौरि नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते॥

*sarvamaṅgalamāṅgalye śive sarvārthasādhike*
*śaraṇye tryambake gauri nārāyaṇi namo 'stu te*

**Padaccheda:** sarva-maṅgala-māṅgalye | śive | sarva-artha-sādhike | śaraṇye | tryambake | gauri | nārāyaṇi | namaḥ | astu | te

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| sarva-maṅgala-māṅgalye | vocative | O auspiciousness *of* all auspicious things (māṅgalya, with long ā, is the abstract form: auspiciousness itself; also the word for the mangalasūtra) |
| śive | vocative | O auspicious one, O Śivā |
| sarva-artha-sādhike | √sādh · fem. voc. sg. | O accomplisher of every aim (artha can stretch to the four puruṣārthas) |
| śaraṇye | vocative (-ya gerundive sense) | O one fit to be taken refuge in |
| tryambake | vocative | O three-eyed one (ambaka = eye; also read as "mother of the three worlds") |
| gauri | vocative | O fair, golden, radiant one |
| nārāyaṇi | vocative | O Nārāyaṇī: Nārāyaṇa's śakti, or the refuge (ayana) of all beings (nāra) |
| namaḥ astu te | √nam (namaḥ) · √as imperative 3rd sg. · te dat. | may there be salutation to you |

**Meaning:** O auspiciousness of all that is auspicious, O Śivā, accomplisher of every aim, O refuge, O three-eyed one, O Gaurī, O Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you.

**Context:** Chapter 11, the Nārāyaṇī-stuti, named for the refrain *nārāyaṇi namo 'stu te*. The devas praise her after Śumbha's death.

**Insights:**
- **It's all vocatives and one tiny verb.** There is no request, no explanation, no "because." Just name after name, the way you keep saying someone's name because you like saying it. After v2 showed how soft her heart is, the devas' only response is to keep calling her. It's a nāmāvali squeezed into 32 syllables.
- **namo 'stu te is "let there be," not "I bow."** *Astu* is an imperative, so it's softer, more like an offering than a statement.

**Reading notes:** Kavitha broke this verse down herself before the explanation, and got about 85% of it right. Corrections:
- *sarva-maṅgala-māṅgalye* isn't "auspicious, auspicious again" as a repetition. Māṅgalya (long ā) is the abstract noun, and the compound works like a genitive: the auspiciousness *of* every auspicious thing.
- *śaraṇye* is more precisely "one *fit to be* taken refuge in," not just "shelter."
- *namostute* splits as *namaḥ + astu + te*, not *namostu + te*.
- Her readings of śive, sarvārthasādhike, tryambake, gauri and both senses of nārāyaṇi were correct.

### 4. śaraṇāgatadīnārta {#v4}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.12 · Metre: anuṣṭup*

शरणागतदीनार्तपरित्राणपरायणे।
सर्वस्यार्तिहरे देवि नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते॥

*śaraṇāgatadīnārtaparitrāṇaparāyaṇe*
*sarvasyārtihare devi nārāyaṇi namo 'stu te*

**Padaccheda:** śaraṇa-āgata-dīna-ārta-paritrāṇa-parāyaṇe | sarvasya | ārti-hare | devi | nārāyaṇi | namaḥ | astu | te

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| śaraṇa-āgata | ā + √gam · ppp. | those who have come for refuge |
| dīna | adj. | the helpless, the wretched |
| ārta | ā + √ṛ · ppp. | the afflicted, those in pain |
| paritrāṇa | pari + √trā · noun | complete rescue, protection all around |
| parāyaṇe | vocative | O one wholly devoted to, absorbed in |
| sarvasya | gen. sg. | of everyone |
| ārti-hare | ā + √ṛ, √hṛ · vocative | O remover of suffering |
| devi nārāyaṇi | vocative | O Devī Nārāyaṇī |
| namaḥ astu te | √nam (namaḥ) · √as imperative 3rd sg. | salutations to you |

**Meaning:** O you who are wholly devoted to rescuing those who come to you for refuge, the helpless and the afflicted; O remover of everyone's suffering, Devī Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you.

**Insights:**
- **Parāyaṇa is flipped.** It's normally the word for the *devotee* (Gītā 9.34, *mat-parāyaṇaḥ*, "be wholly devoted to me"). Here *she* is parāyaṇa, and what she is wholly devoted to is your rescue.
- **The listed qualifications are a mess, on purpose.** *Śaraṇāgata, dīna, ārta*: came for refuge, helpless, hurting. Nothing says "surrendered correctly." Showing up as you are *is* the qualification. The verse doesn't argue with qualification logic. It simply isn't in that courtroom.
- **Paritrāṇa** is the exact word from Gītā 4.8 (*paritrāṇāya sādhūnām*), the reason Krishna descends at all. It's the same divine job description.
- **The scope widens again**: *sarvasya* ārti-hare, everyone's suffering.

**Cross-references:** Gītā 9.34 (*mat-parāyaṇaḥ*); Gītā 7.16 (*ārto jijñāsur arthārthī jñānī ca*, where the hurting devotee is listed first); Gītā 4.8 (*paritrāṇāya sādhūnām*).

**बोधाः:** [5](#r5)

### 5. sarvasvarūpe sarveśe {#v5}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.24 · Metre: anuṣṭup*

सर्वस्वरूपे सर्वेशे सर्वशक्तिसमन्विते।
भयेभ्यस्त्राहि नो देवि दुर्गे देवि नमोऽस्तु ते॥

*sarvasvarūpe sarveśe sarvaśaktisamanvite*
*bhayebhyas trāhi no devi durge devi namo 'stu te*

**Padaccheda:** sarva-svarūpe | sarva-īśe | sarva-śakti-samanvite | bhayebhyaḥ | trāhi | naḥ | devi | durge | devi | namaḥ | astu | te

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| sarva-svarūpe | vocative | O you whose own form is everything |
| sarva-īśe | √īś · īśā, fem. voc. sg. | O ruler of all |
| sarva-śakti-samanvite | vocative | O one fully endowed with every power |
| bhayebhyaḥ | √bhī · bhaya, neut. abl. **pl.** | from fears, every specific one |
| trāhi | √trā · imperative 2nd sg. | protect! save! |
| naḥ | enclitic acc. pl. | us |
| devi durge devi | vocative | O Devī, O Durgā Devī |
| namaḥ astu te | √nam (namaḥ) · √as imperative 3rd sg. | salutations to you |

**Meaning:** O you whose form is everything, ruler of all, possessed of every power, protect us from our fears, O Devī. Durgā Devī, salutations to you.

**Insights:**
- **Three sarvas, then the ask.** She *is* everything, *rules* everything, *has power over* everything, and only then comes the plea. If she is sarva-svarūpa, whatever you fear is also within her form. There is no "outside" where the frightening thing can stand.
- **This is the first real plea in the stotra.** Verses 2–4 are praise and description. Of everything the devas could ask for first, it is "save us from fear."
- ***Naḥ*, "us," not "me."** You never ask alone in this text.
- **The Durgā paradox.** *Dur-gā* is "difficult to approach," like a fortress, yet the opening verse called her *bhakta-sulabhā*, easy for devotees to reach. She is a fortress to everything else and an open door to those who come. The walls are hard so that whoever is inside is safe.
- **It's all her, and you still ask.** "What you fear is also her" doesn't mean the fear is fake or that you should sit in danger. It means nothing is outside her reach. That's why the verse still says *trāhi*: asking is the turning-toward.

**Cross-references:** Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4.2, *dvitīyād vai bhayaṃ bhavati*, "fear comes from a second." If she is everything, there is no second. Gītā 15.15, *mattaḥ smṛtir jñānam apohanaṃ ca*: from him come memory, knowledge, and their loss. It's the same structure as v1 + v2: the giver and the taker of knowing are one. And *smṛti* itself comes from him, so even the act of remembering her (*durge smṛtā*) is her gift.

**बोधाः:** [6](#r6)

### 6. rogān aśeṣān apahaṃsi {#v6}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.29 · Metre: indravajrā*

रोगानशेषानपहंसि तुष्टा
रुष्टा तु कामान् सकलानभीष्टान्।
त्वामाश्रितानां न विपन्नराणां
त्वामाश्रिता ह्याश्रयतां प्रयान्ति॥

*rogān aśeṣān apahaṃsi tuṣṭā*
*ruṣṭā tu kāmān sakalān abhīṣṭān*
*tvām āśritānāṃ na vipan narāṇāṃ*
*tvām āśritā hy āśrayatāṃ prayānti*

**Padaccheda:** rogān | aśeṣān | apahaṃsi | tuṣṭā | ruṣṭā | tu | kāmān | sakalān | abhīṣṭān | tvām | āśritānām | na | vipat | narāṇām | tvām | āśritāḥ | hi | āśrayatām | prayānti

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| rogān aśeṣān | acc. pl. | diseases, every last one |
| apahaṃsi | apa + √han · present 2nd sg. | you strike away, destroy |
| tuṣṭā | √tuṣ · ppp. fem. nom. sg. | when pleased |
| ruṣṭā tu | √ruṣ · ppp. fem. nom. sg. + particle | but when displeased |
| kāmān sakalān abhīṣṭān | abhi + √iṣ (abhīṣṭa, ppp.) · masc. acc. pl. | all desires and wished-for things (the verb carries over: "you destroy") |
| tvām āśritānām narāṇām | ā + √śri · gen. pl. | for people who have taken refuge in you |
| na vipat | nom. | there is no calamity |
| tvām āśritāḥ hi | ā + √śri · nom. pl. | indeed, those who take refuge in you |
| āśrayatām | acc. | the state of *being* a refuge |
| prayānti | pra + √yā · present 3rd pl. | attain, go to |

**Meaning:** When pleased, you destroy all diseases. When displeased, you destroy every cherished desire. For those who take refuge in you there is no calamity, and those who take refuge in you themselves become a refuge for others.

**Insights:**
- **Tuṣṭā / ruṣṭā** is a rhyming pair, pleased and displeased, a small musical hinge. The plain sense is that she has total power in both directions. Devotional reading: given *sadā ārdra-cittā*, even her "destroying your kāmas" can itself be grace, cutting off the wants that would have bound you.
- ***Na vipat*** doesn't mean devotees never face hardship (Suratha and Samādhi still sat through three years of tapas). It means the calamity doesn't get the last word.
- **āśritāḥ → āśrayatām.** This is the best wordplay in the stotra: the sheltered become shelter. Her grace doesn't just protect you, it transforms you into someone others can lean on. The one who came as *dīna-ārta* in v4 leaves able to hold others up.
- ***Narāṇām***: ordinary humans, not devas or ṛṣis.

**Cross-references:** Gītā 9.31, *na me bhaktaḥ praṇaśyati*, "my devotee never perishes."

**बोधाः:** [7](#r7)

### 7. sarvābādhāpraśamanam {#v7}
*Source: Devī Māhātmya 11.39, the closing verse of the Nārāyaṇī-stuti · Metre: anuṣṭup*

सर्वाबाधाप्रशमनं त्रैलोक्यस्याखिलेश्वरि।
एवमेव त्वया कार्यमस्मद्वैरिविनाशनम्॥

*sarvābādhāpraśamanaṃ trailokyasyākhileśvari*
*evam eva tvayā kāryam asmadvairivināśanam*

**Padaccheda:** sarva-ābādhā-praśamanam | trailokyasya | akhila-īśvari | evam | eva | tvayā | kāryam | asmat-vairi-vināśanam

| Word | Root / grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| sarva-ābādhā | compound member | all afflictions, obstacles, troubles |
| praśamanam | pra + √śam · noun | calming, quieting (same root as śānti) |
| trailokyasya | gen. | of the three worlds |
| akhila-īśvari | √īś · fem. voc. sg. | O sovereign of all |
| evam eva | adverb + particle | in just this way, "just as you did" |
| tvayā | instr. | by you |
| kāryam | √kṛ · gerundive | is to be done |
| asmat-vairi-vināśanam | vi + √naś · neut. nom. sg. | the destruction of our enemies |

**Meaning:** O sovereign of all, in just this way may you quiet all the afflictions of the three worlds and destroy our enemies.

**Context:** The closing verse of the Nārāyaṇī-stuti. Śumbha and Niśumbha are gone, and the devas are saying "keep doing exactly this." Right after it, Devī grants her boon and promises to return whenever she is needed.

**Insights:**
- **The bookend.** The opening question asked for an upāya for *kārya*-siddhi. The final verse ends *tvayā **kāryam***: it is to be done by *you*. The answer to "how do I get my work done in Kali-yuga?" is that it's her work, and she does it.
- ***Praśamana*, not just destruction.** Troubles are *quieted*, settled.
- **Inner enemies (devotional reading).** *Asmat-vairi* is often read as the six inner enemies: kāma, krodha, lobha, **moha**, mada, mātsarya. Moha is on the list. In v1 she drags minds into moha; in v7 she is asked to destroy it. The one who binds is asked to unbind.
- ***Kāryam*** has a sweet bossiness to it: not "please, if you like" but "this is yours to do." It's the tone of a child who knows the mother won't say no. You don't file a request with your mother; you cry, and it's understood.

**Reading notes:** Kavitha's alternate readings:
- **trailokyasya with *akhileśvari*** ("O sovereign of the three worlds") instead of with the afflictions. This is **grammatically valid**, because a genitive can hang off either word. The standard reading attaches it to *ābādhā*, partly because *akhila-īśvari* already means "sovereign of all," which makes the other reading slightly redundant. It's a legitimate alternative, just non-standard.
- **evam eva** read as "quiet the afflictions *the way you destroyed* our enemies." This is a **correction.** *Praśamanam* and *vināśanam* are in the same case and both hang off *kāryam*, so they are two parallel requests: quiet the afflictions AND destroy the enemies. A comparison would need a word like *yathā*. Her instinct was still right in spirit: *evam eva* ("just like this") points back to what she just did in the story, slaying Śumbha and Niśumbha. The comparison is to the battle outside the verse, not to anything inside it.

## Metre notes

The viniyoga labels the whole stotra *anuṣṭup*, but that is the conventional label for the stotra, not a strict metrical claim. Because every verse is lifted whole from the Saptaśatī, each keeps its original metre:

| Verse | Metre | Syllables per pāda |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 3, 4, 5, 7 | anuṣṭup | 8 |
| 2 | vasantatilakā | 14 |
| 6 | indravajrā | 11 |

**Vasantatilakā** (v2), pattern `GGLGLLLGLLGLGG`:

```scansion
dur:G ge:G smṛ:L tā:G ha:L ra:L si:L bhī:G ti:L ma:L śe:G ṣa:L jan:G toḥ:G
```

**Indravajrā** (v6), pattern `GGLGGLLGLGG`, in the 11-syllable triṣṭubh family:

```scansion
ro:G gā:G na:L śe:G ṣā:G na:L pa:L haṃ:G si:L tuṣ:G ṭā:G
```

**Related metres:**
- **Upendravajrā** is the same as indravajrā but with a *light* first syllable (`LGLGGLLGLGG`).
- **Upajāti** is a verse that mixes indravajrā and upendravajrā lines. Example: Bhāgavatam 11.2.36, *kāyena vācā manasendriyair vā…*, whose third line (*karoti yad yat sakalaṃ parasmai*) opens light.
- **Gītā 15.15** (*sarvasya cāhaṃ hṛdi sanniviṣṭo*) is **pure indravajrā** in all four lines, the same metre as v6. That's why it sounds different from the surrounding 8-syllable Gītā verses when chanted.
